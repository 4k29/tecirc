There is something meaningful about Instagram—the Meta service that helped create a culture of making everything look good—being the one to do this.

## What is Instants?

The idea is to share an unedited, real moment. You cannot edit it. Unlike Stories, you cannot see who viewed it, it can only be viewed once, and people can react but cannot comment.

The design seems to prioritize sharing a moment casually over putting on a performance. For a long time, Instagram was a place to present something finished: select a photograph, edit it, create a consistent aesthetic, and publish it to followers. Instants takes a different direction. Whether or not a moment is polished, it encourages you to share what is happening right now.

## Why it matters that Meta is doing this

Meta has spent years building public social networks: real-name Facebook, Instagram’s follower economy and carefully composed images, and then algorithm-driven Reels. It has consistently expanded its services around the value of reaching more people.

That is what makes Instants interesting. This is more than adding something similar to BeReal. A company that encouraged the culture of showing and being seen is returning to the idea of sharing something real.

## Ray-Ban Meta

The first thing Instants reminded me of was Ray-Ban Meta. Those glasses also point toward recording real moments much as you see them. Yet those photographs and videos can still be edited and posted afterward.

Instants goes further toward the present moment: no editing, one viewing, and no lasting comments. Ray-Ban Meta is still largely associated with creators and technology enthusiasts rather than everyday use by everyone. Perhaps Instants brings a similar idea to a broader audience at a lower cost.

## Algorithm fatigue in the background

It is difficult to think about Instants without considering the fatigue surrounding social media algorithms.

On Instagram, a post has become something close to a finished creative work. Choose a photograph, edit it, write a caption, and think about views and reactions. As Reels became more central, communicating with friends increasingly gave way to optimizing for strangers.

For many people, social media became a place to be evaluated rather than a place to share daily life. The growth of BeReal, Close Friends, and secondary accounts seems partly a response to that pressure. Instants belongs to the same movement.

## A contradiction

What interests me is that Meta is beginning to design authenticity itself as a user experience: no editing, the present moment, a small audience, disappearing communication.

Being natural or real once seemed like something that happened spontaneously. Now that naturalness is designed by a platform. There is a paradox in a carefully engineered feature asking people to be themselves. Still, as long as communication happens through an app, this is a problem we have to keep confronting.

## A glimpse of what comes after public social media?

Instants is a small feature on its own. Behind it, though, are wider feelings: fatigue with algorithms and polished images, reluctance to post publicly, and a return to smaller communities.

Meta spent years building social networks that let people reach ever larger audiences. With Instants, it seems to shift toward **sharing disappearing moments with a limited group of people**.

That is why it matters that Instagram, which helped create the pressure to look good, is offering a feature where you do not have to.
