Watching the announcement of Apple Watch Series 12 and Apple Watch Ultra 4, I found myself thinking that Apple Watch could become central to Siri AI. Few devices gather information so closely connected to the life and condition of the person wearing them.

![Apple Watch Series 12](../images/notes/AppleWatch-SiriAI-1.jpg)
*Apple Watch Series 12*

![Apple Watch Ultra 4](../images/notes/AppleWatch-SiriAI-2.jpg)
*Apple Watch Ultra 4*

Apple Watch is almost a concentration of personal context.

By personal context, I mean the background information that helps us understand someone: their actions, habits, and circumstances. To offer relevant help, an AI needs more than the question itself.

Apple Watch continuously records activity, sleep, heart rate, and other information. Worn throughout the day, it accumulates clues about someone’s life and changes from their usual patterns.

That makes it a good fit for AI. When asking an AI to help plan something, we explain our available time and what needs doing. For more personal suggestions, we add our habits and recent circumstances.

If a device could supply some of that information with the person’s permission, the conversation would change. We could begin with our circumstances already understood, rather than explain the background every time. Apple Watch matters because it can gather that background close to everyday life.

Audio Intelligence, announced for both Series 12 and Ultra 4, seems to extend that role. Live Rewind lets you check a transcript of the preceding 15 seconds of conversation. With Siri Recap enabled, you can review key points afterward. These features are scheduled for beta availability before the end of the year.

Alongside activity and physical condition, the content of everyday conversations becomes another point of contact with a person’s life.

For example, when you want to look up something you heard, you normally have to remember the words or make a note. Being able to check part of a conversation and ask Siri about it could reduce the work of explaining the situation again.

What I hope to see is these pieces of information connect in more appropriate ways.

An assistant might help plan a day using both your schedule and recent patterns in your life, or find information with reference to a previous conversation. If it detects an unusual change in your physical condition, it might suggest your regular clinic. Context beyond the single sentence you type could make Siri’s suggestions more relevant to your actual life.

Apple has not announced that Siri AI can use all information gathered by Apple Watch across the board. This is my own picture of what could be useful. Measurements also cannot reveal someone’s mood or intentions on their own. The assistant would need to combine them with what the person says and ask when something is unclear.

Still, continuously gathering information that helps understand an individual is a major strength of Apple Watch. Combining information supplied for a question with context accumulated through daily life could broaden what personal AI can help with.

Apple Watch can also deliver information, rather than only gather it.

Check something at the wrist, send a short response, and open more detailed material on an iPhone or Mac. With that division of roles, small everyday tasks could go through the Watch. There is value in receiving contextual help at the same place that gathers the context.

By central, I do not mean that all AI computation happens on the Watch. Apple describes Audio Intelligence as combining the Watch’s chip, models on iPhone, and Private Cloud Compute.

Even when processing is distributed, Apple Watch could remain the central device in contact with someone’s life. Where AI computes and where information about a person originates do not have to be the same place.

People must be able to choose which information is used. Apple says Audio Intelligence features can be enabled individually and are designed without creating or storing audio recordings. As these connections deepen, people must be able to understand the scope of use and stop or correct it when necessary.

This announcement made me reconsider the value of Apple Watch. Activity and health records, everyday conversations, and interactions at the wrist together suggest a device where personal context can gather.

If Siri AI develops into something that supports people with an understanding of their circumstances, Apple Watch’s role could grow considerably. The direction shown by Series 12 and Ultra 4 makes me think it could become central to that experience.
