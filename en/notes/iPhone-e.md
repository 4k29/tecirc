## Introduction

The iPhone e series began with iPhone 16e. While regular models are usually announced in September, the e models arrive about five months later.

The 16e attracted criticism that its price did not match its capabilities. Yet in the year before the 17e announcement, I think it established itself as **a slightly more affordable iPhone with an appealing look**. That is my personal impression, of course. Here I want to consider what this series is for.

## How is e different from SE?

To understand e, it helps to compare it with SE.

SE was built around being small and affordable. The first generation in 2016 put the then-current A9 into an iPhone 5s body. The second generation in 2020 paired the iPhone 8 body with A13 Bionic, and the third generation in 2022 kept that body and added A15 Bionic.

The idea was consistent: a new chip inside an older exterior. As a result, the third-generation SE retained a Home button and a 4.7-inch display and lacked MagSafe. Its chip was current, but its appearance and physical experience felt generations older. It was difficult to escape the impression of a budget version.

The e series changes that thinking. iPhone 16e adopted the 6.1-inch body associated with iPhone 13 and 14, replaced the Home button with Face ID, and included an Action button. iPhone 17e also added MagSafe, absent from both SE and 16e.

Rather than simply recycling an old body, it is positioned as a current-generation device. It leaves out extras such as Camera Control and Dynamic Island while retaining everyday essentials such as satellite connectivity, Ceramic Shield, and a battery that comfortably lasts a day.

If SE was built by adding to an old design, e feels like it was built by subtracting from a current one.

## Where e sits beside the regular and Pro models

iPhone 17 starts at $799 and iPhone 17 Pro at $1,099. iPhone 17e is $599, a $200 difference from the regular model. What is left out helps explain its character.

Face ID uses a notch rather than Dynamic Island. There is a single 48MP Fusion camera, no Camera Control, and a 60Hz display without ProMotion. Those are clear choices.

On the other hand, it retains A19, MagSafe, an Action button, Apple Intelligence support, and 256GB of starting storage. The gap concerns features that are nice to have rather than omissions that make daily use difficult. That is the series’ carefully chosen position.

Recent Pro models have become increasingly specialized for professional uses, including filmmaking, while regular iPhones have reached the capabilities of Pro models from a few generations ago.

## Why the 16e still found an audience

When iPhone 16e was announced, many people felt its specifications did not justify the price. At $599, it was $170 more than the $429 third-generation SE. Compared with Android phones at the same price, it did not look particularly strong on a specification sheet.

Yet some of its appeal lay outside that sheet: the psychological comfort of a price below ¥100,000, Apple’s ecosystem, and the quality of the design. Its size and appearance also had a straightforward appeal. The black and white finishes and clean iPhone 13/14-style body could attract someone simply because they thought it looked good.

## Apple Intelligence and the strategic role of e

Apple Intelligence is important to the e series’ role. On iPhone, it requires at least 8GB of RAM. Support began with iPhone 15 Pro and Pro Max; even the regular iPhone 15 was excluded.

The A18 in iPhone 16e and A19 in iPhone 17e meet those requirements and support Apple Intelligence. From the start, e was designed as an iPhone that could use current AI features.

Providing those capabilities to people who do not buy flagships could increase the share of Apple’s user base with access to Apple Intelligence. Offering a current Apple chip and AI features at $599 is also a strong response to midrange Android phones.

That said, I cannot hide my disappointment at delays in Apple Intelligence development. I hope Apple improves here.

## What the move from SE to e suggests

The shift could be understood as moving away from the category of a budget edition.

SE was a special product at the edge of the range. Apple explicitly places e within the iPhone 16 or iPhone 17 family, describing it as the most affordable member of that family.

Apple has not explained what the letter means. Essential, entry, and everyone all sound plausible. Compared with SE, which stood for Special Edition, another reading emerges. SE emphasized something special; e makes no such claim. It seems to stand for making iPhone available to more people.

## Looking ahead

A slightly more affordable iPhone with an appealing look might be a case where Apple’s intended position and the way people received the product align.

The series is only in its second generation. Differences from the regular model, such as the absence of Dynamic Island, remain. Yet where SE struggled to shake its budget-edition image, e feels designed from the start as an affordable option within the current iPhone family.

The less prominent criticism of 17e compared with 16e may reflect growing understanding of that role. Whether e becomes established as iPhone’s entry line depends on how seriously Apple continues to develop it.
