I have owned Nothing Headphone (1) for almost four months. Apart from exercising and being at school, I wear them nearly all the time. Using them this much has made both their everyday appeal and their longer-term drawbacks clear.

Here is how I ended up buying them after more than six months of indecision, and what I think of the sound, controls, noise cancellation, battery, and wear. I hope it helps if you are considering a pair.

## I thought I did not need headphones

Headphones seemed heavy, bulky, and like one more thing to carry. I thought earbuds were enough. After two years with Beats Fit Pro, though, the strain on my ears and a battery that no longer lasted half a day made me consider a replacement.

Then Nothing Headphone (1) appeared. The recognizable design caught my attention, as did sound developed with KEF. I already liked Nothing, and a review by Yusuke Okawa, whom I admire, helped me decide to buy them in September 2025.

Unfortunately, I had just returned from a week in the United States and had almost no money. I was saving even small train fares. While I saved, AirPods Pro 3 and Nothing Ear (3) arrived, followed by the cheaper Headphone (a) in March 2026. Would earbuds be more convenient? Would the cheaper headphones be enough? The more I thought, the harder it became to decide.

I decided to try a pair in person. I searched electronics shops in Akihabara and Ikebukuro, e-earphone in Tokyo and Osaka, and Yodobashi in Umeda, but struggled to find one. Remembering the KEF connection, I finally went to KEF Music Gallery TOKYO in Omotesando. I tried them and immediately felt drawn to them.

On April 1, 2026, I bought a new pair at the full price of ¥39,800. After all that indecision, they became something I use every day.

## An unusually satisfying thing to own

If you ask whether I am glad I bought them, the answer is an easy yes.

I like the sound and features, but the biggest part is how I feel whenever I see or use them. Seeing them in the morning gives me a little motivation. Putting them on to study or picking them up before going out lifts my mood.

At nearly ¥40,000, good sound and noise cancellation were expected. After four months, though, I feel I bought more than those functions. **Wanting to use something every day has real value.**

After I posted photographs on X, someone who saw them bought a pair and told me they were satisfying to own. Perhaps this feeling is not just mine. Few gadgets have given me this much pleasure simply from having them.

## Balanced sound, with room to adjust the EQ

I am not an audio expert, so I would leave detailed sound evaluation to professional reviewers. As someone listening every day, I find the default tuning balanced and easy to enjoy.

For me, the treble was a little strong and occasionally sharp, while I wanted more bass. I now lower the treble and raise the bass using the Nothing X equalizer.

The EQ offers detailed adjustments. At first, there were too many options for me, so I started with settings shared by the Nothing community. I gradually became comfortable changing them myself, and now appreciate that flexibility.

I mostly listen to Official Hige Dandism. These do not strike me as headphones suited only to one genre; they make a variety of music enjoyable.

## Physical controls

Reviews consistently praised the physical controls. After four months, I agree: they are excellent.

Turn the roller for volume and press it to play or pause. Move the paddle to skip forward or back, and hold it for fast-forward or rewind. I have set the button to change noise control with one press and call the voice assistant with a long press. Being able to customize these assignments is useful.

Most importantly, I can operate them without looking. My fingers naturally find each control when I reach up. The placement feels carefully researched.

Touch controls on many recent headphones can leave me uncertain about where to touch or how to move a finger. When trying WH-1000XM6, for example, I briefly struggled to work out the controls. That rarely happens here. Physical controls are great.

## Natural noise cancellation and transparency

The noise cancellation is strong without feeling excessive. It reduces outside sound well, but I have not experienced an uncomfortable pressure sensation or the unpleasant feeling sometimes associated with ANC. For long listening sessions, the balance suits me.

I also use transparency mode frequently. Surrounding sounds feel natural, and I have not noticed anything awkward. I can switch modes to talk to someone or check my surroundings without taking the headphones off.

The design draws attention, but the everyday functions are well considered too.

## A reliable two days of battery

Battery life is excellent. Even with all-day use, I can reliably get two full days. With my usage, there is no need to charge every night.

I normally connect only to my phone and have not experienced a single dropout or connection problem. I have not used multipoint or the call microphone, so I am not evaluating those here.

When going out, I carry the included case in my backpack. Unless I am at school or somewhere similar, they spend more time on my head than in the case.

## Long sessions can still be tiring

There are drawbacks. Clamping force and compatibility with glasses are the main comfort issues.

I rarely notice the weight in daily use, but the clamp is slightly strong. Eventually it becomes tiring. With glasses, the arms can press around my ears and hurt. That is a broader issue with over-ear headphones, but if you regularly wear glasses, try these on first.

They also get hot in summer. Ear pads covering the whole ear are not comfortable for long periods on a hot day. I do not use them for exercise. I still wear them for much of the day, but that does not mean they are comfortable every minute.

## Do they scratch easily?

Because of their shape, the corners of the ear cups knock together when I wear them around my neck. This can leave black marks. Those wipe off, but the aluminum corners have developed small scratches and spots where the surface appears worn away.

The aluminum and transparent plastic make scratches noticeable. A small scratch from dropping them once hurt my feelings more than its size might suggest.

Some wear is inevitable with daily use. Still, many people choose these headphones for their design, so I would appreciate better scratch resistance.

## After four months

At ¥39,800, these are not cheap. I spent more than six months deciding, yet now wish I had bought them sooner.

The sound is balanced and adjustable. The physical controls are easy to use, ANC and transparency feel natural, the battery lasts, and the phone connection has been reliable. As everyday headphones, they give me very little trouble.

Pain with glasses, summer heat, and scratches do bother me. Even accounting for those, I am very satisfied. Seeing them in the morning still gives me the same small boost after four months.

I can strongly recommend them. If you want headphones that are enjoyable to own as well as capable, try seeing a pair in person. If you have a question this review does not cover, feel free to ask me by DM on X.
