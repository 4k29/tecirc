iPhone Duo runs iOS. Yet after looking at its announced features and interface, I feel uneasy about using the same name as the conventional iPhone. It builds on iOS, brings in strengths from iPadOS, and adds ways of working specific to a foldable device. That is what I want to think about here.

![iPhone Duo](../images/notes/iphone-duo-os-1.jpg)
*iPhone Duo*

Duo supports Split View for two apps side by side and two windows from the same app. Its Dock and controls sit along the side, and the interface changes with the folded state. The larger screen comes with a redesign of how the device is operated.

![Multitasking on iPhone Duo](../images/notes/iphone-duo-os-2.jpg)
*Multitasking on iPhone Duo*

This combines the accessibility of iOS with the workspace of iPadOS. Keep it closed for a quick interaction, then open it to compare information or refer to one app while working in another. The same device can change its working area to suit the task.

The difference goes beyond screen size. You can use the outer display to frame a photograph taken with the rear camera, or show a preview to the person being photographed. Folding the device and setting it down also allows photography without a tripod.

How it folds, how it rests, and who looks at which screen all become part of software design. Duo’s novelty lies in combining the strengths of iOS and iPadOS with these foldable-specific interactions.

That brings me to iPadOS.

In 2019, Apple gave the iPad’s operating system its own name. Its announcement described a system built on the same foundation as iOS, with features that take advantage of the iPad’s larger display and versatility. The name represented a distinct iPad experience.

Sharing a technical foundation with iOS did not prevent iPadOS from having its own identity. The reason for separating the names was what the device could do and how people used it.

Apply that reasoning to Duo, and there is a case for considering another name. It moves between portability and a larger workspace, uses inner and outer displays differently, and supports interactions designed around resting a partly folded device on a surface. Its software responds to conditions different from either a conventional iPhone or an iPad.

A distinct name could help buyers understand those differences. It could also signal to developers that adapting to screen size is only part of the job: folding and multiple displays deserve attention too. A name alone will not create more compatible apps, but it can communicate how Apple positions the software.

Of course, the conclusion need not match iPadOS. The iPad is a separate product category; Duo is an iPhone model. Keeping the name iOS emphasizes continuity and familiar interactions. Adding another name could create unnecessary confusion about compatibility and operation.

So I do not think more features alone justify a rename. The question is whether Duo’s particular interactions are central to its value.

From the announcement, folding support seems more than an extra. Changing the interface and controls with the device’s state is an important part of its design. Just as iPadOS names a distinct iPad experience, I think there could be value in naming Duo’s experience too.

I cannot yet judge how well it works on real hardware, or how consistently compatible apps deliver that experience. Still, saying it is based on iOS does not fully explain what makes Duo different. An operating system combining iOS, iPadOS, and foldable-specific uses might deserve its own name.
